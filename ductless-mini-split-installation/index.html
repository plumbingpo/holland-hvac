Mini splits exist for the rooms central air forgets. The garage workshop, the sunroom that bakes by noon, the addition the ducts never reached, the Holland house that was built without ductwork at all: each of these can be heated and cooled on its own by one or more wall-mounted heads. A slim line set carries refrigerant, power, and condensate drainage through a single three-inch wall penetration to the outdoor unit, so there is no duct system losing energy along the way. The inverter compressor speeds up and slows down with the load rather than cycling hard, which keeps the room steady, quiet, and cheap to run. In winter, the same equipment works as a cold-climate heat pump, not a cooling unit pretending to heat.

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Holland HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk the space with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since a weeping flare is the classic mini split failure. Condensate is drained or pumped reliably, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings explained in plain terms. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion creates a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing lukewarm air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall leaves a water stain the first humid week.

What separates good ductless work from bad is discipline: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Holland HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and testing each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a place to improvise. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • In winter, expect defrost pauses and light steam from the outdoor unit; solid ice means call us
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow, leaves, and grass clippings in every season
  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • On humid summer days, confirm the drain line outside is dripping as it should
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
