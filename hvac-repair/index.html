Ask any HVAC technician in Holland and you will hear the same thing: equipment seldom breaks down on a mild day. It waits for a heat wave or a hard freeze. What makes repair tricky is that very different faults look identical from the thermostat. If the system runs but cannot hold temperature, the cause could be a weak run capacitor, pitted contactor points, a refrigerant leak, a fouled flame sensor, or just a filter packed so tight that airflow collapses. If it will not start at all, look to a float switch tripped by a clogged condensate line, a dead transformer, a faulty thermostat, or a control board that no longer sequences the equipment.

Warning Signs for HVAC Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rising utility costs even though the house feels worse
  • A humming outdoor unit that never kicks on
  • Short cycling, where the system starts and stops without ever reaching the setpoint
  • Vents blowing warm when you want cool, or cool when you want heat
  • Barely any air coming out of the vents
  • A circuit breaker that keeps tripping when the system starts
  • Unfamiliar sounds from the equipment, like squealing, grinding, or banging
  • Water pooling around the indoor equipment, often from a clogged condensate drain

HVAC Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of grass, leaves, and shrubs so the condenser coil can breathe
  • Have the system professionally serviced twice a year, once before summer and once before winter
  • Run a vinegar flush through the condensate line each spring to prevent clogs and float switch trips
  • Swap in a fresh filter every one to three months; a clogged one starves the whole system of air
  • During heat waves and cold snaps, set the thermostat a few degrees closer to the outdoor temperature to ease the load
  • Do not ignore new sounds; catching a noisy motor before it locks up saves real money
  • Keep supply and return registers open and unblocked in every room
